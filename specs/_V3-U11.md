## What the corrected sentences claim, in my own words (written from the pages before any Lean)

- **§5.4 (draft p14).** The served text said the reflection s ↔ 1 − s has "fixed locus Re(s) = 1/2" — false: s ↦ 1 − s fixes only the point 1/2. The draft says instead: (5.2) ζ(s) = χ(s)ζ(1 − s), with χ(s) = 2^s π^(s−1) sin(πs/2) Γ(1 − s) now printed, is carried to itself by s ↔ 1 − s (apply it at 1 − s and the two instances are one equation, because χ(s)χ(1 − s) = 1), and that reflection has the single fixed point s = 1/2. The LINE Re s = 1/2 enters through a second symmetry: ζ(s̄) = conj ζ(s). Composing the two, a zero ρ gives zeros 1 − ρ and ρ̄, hence 1 − ρ̄ — the mirror image of ρ in the line, which is exactly the set fixed by s ↦ 1 − s̄. So the non-trivial zeros (not the trivial ones: −2 ↦ 3 is not a zero) are symmetric about the critical line. Why the paper holds it [CV]: the functional equation plus Schwarz reflection, standard; nothing about RH is claimed.
- **§7.1 (draft p17–18).** The served text classified all elements of SL(2, ℤ) into three types by |tr|; but ±I have |tr| = 2 and fix everything, so they are not parabolic. The draft restricts to "elements other than ±I" and adds "γ ≠ ±I" to the Parabolic row. The claim: every non-central γ is exactly one of hyperbolic (|tr| > 2), elliptic (|tr| < 2), parabolic (|tr| = 2); the types are conjugacy-class properties ("Conjugacy class" header); a parabolic element fixes a single boundary point, a cusp. Why the paper holds it: the fixed points of z ↦ (az + b)/(cz + d) solve cz² + (d − a)z − b = 0, whose discriminant is tr² − 4; zero discriminant with γ non-scalar gives one real double root (or ∞ when c = 0), no point of ℍ; for SL(2, ℤ) that root (a − d)/(2c) is rational, so the point is a cusp of X(1) (whose cusp set is ℚ ∪ {∞}, one orbit; §7.2 n_cusp = 1).
