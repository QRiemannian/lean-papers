## Own words, from the print (written before the specs were opened)

- **N2a (p19).** Hecke's period of the Eisenstein series over one period of the golden geodesic equals the closed form (5.2a), 5^{s/2} Γ(s/2)² ζ(s) L(s, χ5) / (Γ(s) ζ(2s)), which is [CV]. The print's claim is that this period vanishes at every nontrivial zero ρ of ζ **with Re ρ ≥ 1/2**, and, under RH, at all nontrivial zeros. The qualifier is the denominator's guard. For Re ρ ≥ 1/2 we have Re 2ρ ≥ 1, so ζ(2ρ) ≠ 0, and 2ρ ≠ 1 because ζ(1/2) ≠ 0. Γ(ρ/2) and Γ(ρ) are finite and nonzero, since Re ρ > 0. L(·, χ5) is entire. So the vanishing comes through the factor ζ(ρ) = 0, as the print says ("they enter (5.2a) through the factor ζ(s)"). Below Re 1/2, ζ(2ρ) could vanish and cancel the zero. So the print carries the Re ρ ≥ 1/2 reading, plus "all of them" under RH. Every echo (p02, p03, p21, p26) is the critical-line special case.
- **N3 (p09, p11).** With systole(X(1)) = 4 log φ (3.5), the framework reads φ off the systole through φ = exp(¼ · systole); §3.2 restates the metric coordinate as log φ = ¼ · systole. It is a two-form identity, given the systole's value.
- **N5a (p19 caption).** The figure plots |closed form| at s = 1/2 + it, 0 ≤ t ≤ 40. A **node** is a point where the plotted modulus goes to zero, the plot's dips to the floor of the log scale. The caption marks the ζ-zeros and the L-zeros as nodes, and the cross at t = 0 as "the one further node", where the pole of ζ(2s) at s = 1/2 sends the period to zero. "Every zero of ζ_ℚ(√5)(s) = ζ(s)L(s, χ5) is a node": a node lives on the plotted line, so this is read on the critical line. The t = 0 node **is in N5a's scope**: it is the caption's own sentence. Its vanishing is by the pole (the numerator stays bounded and the denominator blows up), not by a zero of ζ·L. "Further" asserts that t = 0 is not a ζ·L zero; "one" asserts that there is no other non-ζ·L node in the window.
