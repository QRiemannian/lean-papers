## Strength — the readings I weighed
1. **"the reflections of the 4-cycle" = four, not two.** The sentence calls the 4-cycle "that rotation". In T_d that cycle is an S₄ rotoreflection, so the sentence is speaking the square's language, and the square's reflections are four (V₁ (a)). The strict T_d-mirror reading would name only σ₁, σ₂. Under that reading σ₃, σ₄ are still entailed by the same sentence: each is σ₁ or σ₂ composed with a rotation, and the sentence asserts rotation invariance. An anti-automorphism composed with an automorphism is an anti-automorphism, and the same holds for (ii)(a). So the four-conjunct statements say nothing the sentence does not, under either reading. Not stronger.
2. **"carry … to one another" = maps the set onto itself, not a derangement.** σ₃ fixes (3.2) and (3.4), and σ₄ fixes (3.3) and (3.5) (table 1). A reading where every relation must move to a *different* one would make the clause false for the edge reflections. The print does not force that reading: "to one another" is the idiom for permuting among themselves, the same set-level sense N2a's "invariant" carries. The iff states the set-level content. Recorded, no drift.
3. **Semantic iff versus literal permutation (i).** The iff over all algebras is the universal-property form of "σ extends to an anti-automorphism of the presented algebra mapping the relations onto themselves". The literal permutation (table 1) is stronger and also true. This is the N2a convention (V1/V2/V3 signed there). It is not weaker in any way the print distinguishes.
4. **(b): matrices and ħ ≠ 0.** "Only" is a negative claim about the presentation. Any witness algebra exhibits it, and pinning the carrier to `Matrix (Fin 2) (Fin 2) ℂ` makes the existential concrete (rz_81 (b)'s house form). The witness exists for every ħ ≠ 0 (above), so the concretization adds a proof burden but no false content. ∀ ħ ≠ 0 is the paper's arbitrary nonzero ℏ, and an ∃ ħ form would be weaker. At ħ = 0, R⁻ = R₋ and "only" is vacuous, so the exclusion is right. (b) is reflection-free, and (a), inside the same theorem, is what ties it to the reflections. Given (a), it is equivalent to the per-reflection form. Not stronger, not weaker.
5. **(ii)(a) stays (V₁ (d)).** (i) and (ii)(a) are one fact through op. I checked this: table 2's permutation is table 1's, and R in Aᵐᵒᵖ at op u is R⁻(u). My call is on faithfulness: "as automorphisms they reverse the commutators' sign only up to the order of the product" is a printed assertion in its own words, and (a) states exactly that assertion — the automorphism images are R⁻ — with sign and order inline. (i) states the other clause in the anti-automorphism's words. Each printed clause has its own statement. Dropping (a) would leave theorem (ii) as "R⁻ ≠ R₋", which is no longer about automorphisms. Keep.
6. **Universe and binders.** (i): `{A : Type*}` at the theorem (N2a's form). (ii): `∀ {A : Type u} [Ring A] [Algebra ℂ A]` inside the conjunction, with `universe u` in the unit header (rz_81's form; `#check` shows `rz_N2b_reflections_sign_up_to_order.{u}`, and the probe instantiates `.{0}`). Both are universe-polymorphic.
7. **Parentheses.** ↔ (20) is looser than ∧ (35). Probe: destructuring each theorem into exactly four `(_ = _ ∧ _ = _ ∧ _ = _ ∧ _ = _) ↔ (_ = _ ∧ _ = _ ∧ _ = _ ∧ _ = _)` blocks elaborates (for (i) all four; for (ii)(a) the first and the last), the right sides of (i) typecheck as equations in `Aᵐᵒᵖ`, and (b) has the shape `∀ ħ, ħ ≠ 0 → ∃ E S H D : Matrix …, (4-conjunction) ∧ ¬ (4-conjunction)`. `#check`'s pretty-print shows every iff parenthesised inside the ∧ chain. `(-(Complex.I * (ħ : ℂ))) • x` pretty-prints as `-(Complex.I * ↑ħ) • x`: neg binds tighter than `•`, so it is the same term.
