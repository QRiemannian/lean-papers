## 3 · Formal reading
- F² acts on the boundary ∂ℍ = ℝ ∪ {∞} as the real Möbius map z ↦ (2z + 1)/(z + 1), with the projective conventions at −1 (↦ ∞) and ∞ (↦ 2/1 = 2). In mathlib: ℝ ∪ {∞} is `OnePoint ℝ`, and GL(2, ℝ) acts on it by Möbius transformations (`OnePoint.instGLAction`), so F² is taken as the element of `GL (Fin 2) ℝ` with matrix `!![2, 1; 1, 1]` (det 1 ≠ 0).
- "Its two fixed points … are φ and −1/φ" is read as the full set statement: the fixed-point set of F² on `OnePoint ℝ` is exactly {φ, −1/φ} — φ and −1/φ are fixed, they are distinct ("two"), and nothing else (∞ included) is fixed. A one-way reading ("φ and −1/φ are fixed") would be weaker than "its two fixed points are".
- φ is `Real.goldenRatio`; −1/φ is written `-1 / Real.goldenRatio` as printed (it equals `Real.goldenConj` by `Real.inv_goldenRatio`; L may use either internally).
- Arithmetic behind it (for L, not a claim): the finite fixed points are the roots of z² − z − 1 (`fixpointPolynomial` of the element: C 1 · X² + C (1 − 2) · X − C 1); ∞ is not fixed since the (1,0) entry is 1 ≠ 0 (`OnePoint.smul_infty_eq_self_iff`).
