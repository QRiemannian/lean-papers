## The cusp-reading decision (RZ-41b)
**The ℚ ∪ {∞} clause is what "(cusp)" means here; it stays in Lean, not a docstring reading.** The paper defines no cusp formally; its usage fixes the meaning. It speaks of "the cusp" of X(1) with n_cusp = 1 (§7.2, draft p18) — the one Γ-orbit of cusps, whose points in ∂ℍ are ℙ¹(ℚ) = ℚ ∪ {∞} — and it says outright, at §16 (draft p37), "rational numbers correspond to the parabolic (cusp) data … [CV]". So "Fixes a single boundary point (cusp)" names that point as a cusp of the modular group, i.e. a point of ℚ ∪ {∞}. The clause is not stronger than the print: it does not say the point is ∞ (that would overclaim — e.g. [[1,0],[1,1]] fixes 0), not that every rational is fixed by some parabolic, not cusp widths (the spec's §6 lists these exclusions). Under the other textbook definition (a cusp is a boundary point fixed by a parabolic element) the parenthesis would be a tautology, and the clause would be the standard identification of the cusps of SL(2, ℤ) with ℙ¹(ℚ) — still the paper's own usage at p37. Either way, nothing beyond the parenthesis's content.
