## The paper's claim in my own words (written before the Lean was read; kept as written)

- **N2a (p19, the sentence after (5.2a)).** Hecke's period of the Eisenstein series of X(1) over one period of the golden geodesic equals the closed form (5.2a), F(s) = 5^{s/2} Γ(s/2)² ζ(s) L(s, χ₅) / (Γ(s) ζ(2s)). The claim is that F "vanishes at every nontrivial zero ρ with Re ρ ≥ 1/2 — under RH, at all of them". F is meromorphic, with Γ(s)⁻¹ and ζ(2s)⁻¹ in it, so "vanishes at ρ" means F has a zero at ρ: F is holomorphic near ρ and tends to 0 there. The paper's reason is in the next clause: the zeros "enter (5.2a) through the factor ζ(s)". ζ(ρ) = 0 is a factor. The other factors stay finite at ρ. Γ(ρ/2) is finite because Re ρ > 0, and 5^{ρ/2} is finite. L(·, χ₅) is entire. Γ(s)⁻¹ is entire. ζ(2ρ) ≠ 0 because Re 2ρ ≥ 1. That last fact is why the qualifier Re ρ ≥ 1/2 is there: below 1/2, a zero of ζ(2s) could cancel the zero of ζ(s). Under RH every nontrivial zero has Re = 1/2, so the qualified statement covers all of them. The point s = 1/2 (2s = 1, the pole of ζ(2s)) is not a zero of ζ, since ζ(1/2) ≈ −1.46. The caption treats it separately, as "the one further node" (N5a's territory, not N2a's).
- **N3 (p09 after (3.5); p11).** systole(X(1)) = 4 log φ (3.5). The framework reads φ off it "through the identity φ = exp(¼ · systole)", and §3.2 gives the metric coordinate "log φ = ¼ · systole". Given the systole's value, this is the inverse reading of (3.5): an identity, not a discovery. The paper presents it as an identity.

## The questions the brief set

- **Is "vanishes" a limit on a punctured neighbourhood?** Yes: `Filter.Tendsto heckePeriodClosedForm (nhdsWithin ρ {ρ}ᶜ) (nhds 0)` (Challenge :60, :63; elaborated, probe). The pointwise value F(ρ) is never used. That value would be 0 trivially in Lean (0 · x / y), and the limit form shuts that route.
- **The 2ρ = 1 branch (ρ = 1/2).** Under the hypotheses, ρ = 1/2 is **not a possible zero**: ζ(1/2) = −1.46035450881 ≠ 0 (guard). The branch is mathematically unreachable. Lean cannot rule it out at the pin, because no ζ(1/2) ≠ 0 lemma exists there, so the proof handles it uniformly instead (:105–131). N(s) → N(1/2) = 0 is the (false) hypothesis instance. ζ(2s)⁻¹ → 0 comes from the genuine `riemannZeta_residue_one`, composed with s ↦ 2s and carried into 𝓝[≠] 1. That step is honest analysis (the pole of ζ at 1), not a junk value. **No conclusion the paper means rests on this branch.** At every genuine nontrivial zero 2ρ ≠ 1 holds, and the other branch carries the theorem. The branch is not a loophole. It proves the theorem in a case the hypotheses cannot reach, and it uses no false lemma to do it. The paper's own statement about s = 1/2 (the pole sends F to 0 while ζ(1/2)·L(1/2, χ₅) ≠ 0) is N5a's second conjunct and its declared gap, not N2a's.
- **Is the hypothesis the paper's?** Yes. "Nontrivial zero" is the conjunction ζ(ρ) = 0 ∧ ¬∃ n : ℕ, ρ = −2(n+1) ∧ ρ ≠ 1, which is mathlib's `RiemannHypothesis` vocabulary. "Re ρ ≥ 1/2" is `(1/2 : ℝ) ≤ ρ.re`, real division. With Re ρ ≥ 1/2, the trivial-zero clause and ρ ≠ 1 are redundant (ζ(1) is a nonzero junk value, Nonvanishing.lean:409–410). Redundancy is not vacuity. The conjunction is satisfied by every zero on the line, which exist. Mathlib carries no witness, but the statement is not empty.
- **The RH conjunct** uses mathlib's `RiemannHypothesis` (not a local restatement) and concludes the limit at every nontrivial zero: "under RH, at all of them".
- **N3 against L:MS-22.** The hypothesis binder text is identical to `ms_22_halfSystole` (`ModularSurface/Now/Challenge.lean:206`): `(systole : ℝ) (h : systole = 4 * Real.log Real.goldenRatio)`. That is (3.5)'s value as a hypothesis, and the value itself stays with L:MS-06 = L:RZ-06b. **N3 carries content beyond MS-22, though thin.** MS-22 proves systole/2 = 2 log φ, its four-decimal value, and systole ≠ 2 log φ. N3 proves the inversion φ = exp(systole/4), which needs exp ∘ log on φ > 0, together with the printed log-form. Neither row implies the other. N3 is a tautology up to that one positivity step. It does not dress up anything the paper does not claim: the paper itself calls it "the identity", and the substantive claim (the systole's value) is kept as a hypothesis on purpose. The paper's strength, no more and no less.
