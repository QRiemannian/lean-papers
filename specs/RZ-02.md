## 3 · Formal reading
- **Objects.** F is the integer matrix `!![1, 1; 1, 0] : Matrix (Fin 2) (Fin 2) ℤ`. The symbol F² is read literally as the matrix power `F ^ 2`; the print asserts it equals `!![2, 1; 1, 1]` ("F² is the square of the Fibonacci matrix F", "Its square F² = (2 1; 1 1)").
- **SL(2, ℤ).** `Matrix.SpecialLinearGroup (Fin 2) ℤ`; membership of an integer 2×2 matrix is exactly det = 1. "In SL(2, ℤ) … realized by F²" is read as: there is an element of `Matrix.SpecialLinearGroup (Fin 2) ℤ` whose underlying matrix is `!![2, 1; 1, 1]`.
- **Trace / determinant.** `Matrix.trace`, `Matrix.det`, over ℤ.
- **Discriminant (convention named).** "Discriminant" of a matrix is not a mathlib notion; the paper fixes its own convention two equations later, at (2.7), p08: "tr(F²)² − 4 = 3² − 4 = 5", i.e. the discriminant of the characteristic polynomial λ² − (tr)λ + det, which is tr² − 4·det. The formal reading takes the discriminant of `Matrix.charpoly` in the sense of `Polynomial.discr`; for a degree-two polynomial mathlib's `Polynomial.discr_of_degree_eq_two` gives coeff₁² − 4·coeff₀·coeff₂, which for the charpoly X² − 3X + 1 (via `Matrix.charpoly_fin_two`) is 9 − 4 = 5 — the same number with the same sign as the paper's tr² − 4·det. The spec offers the charpoly form as the statement and the tr² − 4·det form as the bridge; both are stated so V₁ can pick (they are equivalent by the two named lemmas).
- **No hypotheses, no domains.** Closed computation over ℤ.
