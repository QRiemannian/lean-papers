## The paper's claim in my own words (written before the Lean was read; kept as written)

What chi_5 is (§9.2, the sentence after (9.4)): chi_5 is the Kronecker symbol (5/.), named by the paper as THE unique real primitive Dirichlet character of conductor 5. It is the quadratic character of Q(sqrt5): by §9.4 its value at a prime p is +1 if p splits in Q(sqrt5), -1 if p is inert, 0 at the ramified prime p = 5. As a function on Z/5Z: 0 at 0, +1 at the squares 1 and 4, -1 at the non-squares 2 and 3.

What the paper claims about L(1, chi_5): (9.4) the Dedekind zeta of Q(sqrt5) factors as zeta(s)·L(s,chi_5); then "the class-number formula gives" (9.5) L(1,chi_5) = 2 log(phi)/sqrt5 ≈ 0.4304. The value is restated as (9.8) and derived in (9.9) from Dirichlet's class-number formula for a real quadratic field, L(1,chi_d) = 2 h log(eps)/sqrt(d_K), with h = 1, eps = phi (fundamental unit, norm -1), d_K = 5 — no pi in this real-quadratic formula. L(1,chi_5) is the genuine L-value at s = 1 (the series converges conditionally there since chi_5 is non-principal; it is the value of the entire L-function at 1). The decimal is printed to four places: 0.4304.

The weight-two sentence (§9.5, (9.7)–(9.9)): the systolic element F^2 = [[2,1],[1,1]] has multiplier phi^2 and norm N = phi^4; its Selberg spectral weight log N / (N^{1/2} - N^{-1/2}) = log(phi^4)/(phi^2 - phi^-2) = 4 log phi / sqrt5 = 2·L(1,chi_5) ≈ 0.8608. The weight is a DIFFERENT quantity from L(1,chi_5) and equals exactly twice it; the 2 is the unit index log(eps+)/log(eps) with eps+ = phi^2 (since N(phi) = -1). The proof the paper means: compute phi^2 - phi^-2 = sqrt5 (the norm-gap), log(phi^4) = 4 log phi, then compare with the class-number value 2 log phi/sqrt5. So the "2" claim rests on (a) the explicit weight algebra and (b) the L-value of (9.5)/(9.9).
