## 3 · Formal reading
- **Group.** SL(2, ℤ) = `Matrix.SpecialLinearGroup (Fin 2) ℤ` (scoped notation `SL(2, ℤ)` under `MatrixGroups`); an element is used through its coercion to `Matrix (Fin 2) (Fin 2) ℤ`.
- **"Hyperbolic" (convention named).** Mathlib defines it by the discriminant: `Matrix.IsHyperbolic m := 0 < m.discr` (FinTwo.lean:137), with `Matrix.discr A = A.charpoly.discr` and `Matrix.discr_fin_two : A.discr = A.trace ^ 2 - 4 * A.det`. `Matrix.GeneralLinearGroup.IsHyperbolic g` is an `abbrev` for `g.val.IsHyperbolic` (FinTwo.lean:224). The paper's "hyperbolic element" is the standard notion for the action on ℍ (two fixed points on the boundary), whose textbook characterisation at det 1 is |tr| > 2. The formal reading takes mathlib's discriminant definition as the definition of "hyperbolic"; clause (i) then is the printed half of the bridge, "discriminant > 0 ⇒ |tr| > 2 at det 1" — a genuine statement under mathlib's definition, not a tautology. The hyperbolicity is read over ℤ (the order on ℤ); since tr² − 4 is an integer, positivity over ℤ and over ℝ coincide, so nothing is lost against the SL(2, ℝ) reading.
- **"Integer trace".** The trace of an element of SL(2, ℤ) is an integer by type (`Matrix.trace` of an ℤ-matrix is in ℤ); no separate clause is needed.
- **"The smallest available trace is t = 3" (reading named).** Trace is read in absolute value: the sentence's own premise is |t| > 2, and traces of hyperbolic elements are unbounded below (−g is hyperbolic with trace −tr g, by `Matrix.isHyperbolic_neg_iff`), so a minimum of the signed trace does not exist. The formal clause is `IsLeast` of the set of |tr| over hyperbolic elements, at 3 — this contains both the lower bound (every hyperbolic |tr| ≥ 3) and attainment ("available"; "realized by F²"). An equivalent PSL-style reading — the least positive trace of a hyperbolic element is 3 — is the same set restricted to t > 0; V₁ may choose it.
- **Domains / hypotheses.** None beyond g ∈ SL(2, ℤ).

## 5 · Intended Lean statement
**Prose.** (i) If an element g of SL(2, ℤ) is hyperbolic (mathlib: its 2×2 discriminant is positive), then |tr g| > 2; the same for its image in GL(2, ℤ) under `toGL`. (ii) The least value of |tr g| over hyperbolic g ∈ SL(2, ℤ) is 3: every hyperbolic g has |tr g| ≥ 3, and some hyperbolic g has |tr g| = 3.

The print states (i) in one direction ("a hyperbolic element has integer trace with |t| > 2"), so the row statement is the implication. The converse (|t| > 2 ⇒ discriminant > 0 at det 1) is the other half of the definitional bridge between mathlib's discriminant definition and the standard |t| > 2 characterisation; it is offered to L as a helper lemma for `Solution.lean`, not as a row statement, so the row says no more than the print.
