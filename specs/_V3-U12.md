## The paper's claims in my own words (written from the pages before any Lean)

- **N1 (p08, §2.3).** Attach to a hyperbolic trace t > 2 the length ℓ(t) = 2 arccosh(t/2). ℓ grows with t. It does not grow with the discriminant: the paper pairs d = 12 with trace 4 (ℓ ≈ 2.634) and d = 8 with trace 6 (ℓ ≈ 3.525), so the larger discriminant has the shorter length. The pairing is the one the previous sentence uses for t = 3 ↔ d = 5: t² − 4 = d·u², with t the smallest such trace and d the field's (fundamental) discriminant — 12 = 4² − 4 for ℚ(√3) (unit 2 + √3), 32 = 6² − 4 = 8·2² for ℚ(√2) (the norm-one unit 3 + 2√2 = (1 + √2)²). Why the paper thinks it true: arccosh is increasing, and the two pairs are an explicit counterexample to monotonicity in d.
- **N2a (p09–p10, §3).** Each of (3.2)–(3.5) has the shape [X, Y] = iℏ Z·W with (X, Y, Z, W) running cyclically along Ê → Ŝ → Ĥ → D̂ → Ê. Substituting that rotation into the four relations sends (3.2) → (3.3) → (3.4) → (3.5) → (3.2), so the relation SET is carried onto itself: "invariant under that rotation", hence under the order-4 cyclic group it generates. Why true: the relations were written as the four cyclic shifts of one relation; invariance is that construction read back. The reflection clause (sign reversal) and "not under all of T_d" are separate claims, not this leg.
- **N4b (p19, §7.4).** With Λ(s) = π^(−s/2)Γ(s/2)ζ(s) (no s(s − 1) factor) and φ(s) = Λ(2s − 1)/Λ(2s), |φ| = 1 on Re s = 1/2 — unitarity of the scattering on the continuous spectrum. Why true (the [CV] reason, standard): on the line, 2s − 1 = 1 − conj(2s), so the functional equation Λ(1 − w) = Λ(w) and the reflection Λ(conj w) = conj Λ(w) give Λ(2s − 1) = conj Λ(2s); Λ(2s) ≠ 0 because Re 2s = 1 (ζ has no zeros on Re = 1, Γ none in Re > 0). The paper says "the line"; it does not single out s = 1/2, where φ is a removable singularity (the two Λ poles at 0 and 1 cancel to a limit −1).
