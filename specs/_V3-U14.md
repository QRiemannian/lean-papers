## The paper's claims in my own words (written from the pages before any Lean)

- **The relations.** [Ê, Ŝ] = iℏ ĤD̂ (3.2), [Ŝ, Ĥ] = iℏ D̂Ê (3.3), [Ĥ, D̂] = iℏ ÊŜ (3.4), [D̂, Ê] = iℏ ŜĤ (3.5). Each is [X, Y] = iℏ ZW with (X, Y, Z, W) running cyclically along Ê → Ŝ → Ĥ → D̂ → Ê. The rotation of the 4-cycle permutes them (N2a, not this leg).
- **(i) — the anti-automorphism half.** Take a reflection σ of the 4-cycle (a symmetry of the square Ê–Ŝ–Ĥ–D̂ that reverses its orientation). Extend it to a map that is linear over the scalars (so iℏ stays iℏ) and reverses products. Then [X, Y] = iℏ ZW goes to σY·σX − σX·σY = iℏ σW·σZ, i.e. [σY, σX] = iℏ σW σZ. Because σ reverses the cyclic order, (σY, σX, σW, σZ) is again a cyclic quadruple of the square, so the image is again one of (3.2)–(3.5): the relation set is carried onto itself. **Why the paper thinks it true:** reversing the orientation of the cycle and reversing the order of products cancel; the relation set is the four cyclic shifts of one relation, and a reflection maps cyclic shifts to cyclic shifts once the products are reversed.
- **(ii) — the automorphism half.** Extend σ instead to an order-keeping automorphism. Then [X, Y] = iℏ ZW goes to [σX, σY] = iℏ σZ σW. Since σ reverses the cycle, [σX, σY] is minus the set's commutator on that pair, and σZ σW is the set's product in the reversed order. So each image is a relation of the set with the commutator's sign reversed AND the right-hand product's order reversed. "Only up to the order of the product": with the order kept, the sign-reversed relations are not what you get. **Why true:** the same orientation reversal, now with nothing to absorb it in the product, shows up as a sign on the commutator and a reversal on the right.
- What the leg does not claim: anything about the rotation (N2a), "not under all of T_d" (N2c), the unspecified pairs [Ê, Ĥ], [Ŝ, D̂] (§13.3), or that a non-degenerate realization exists (the "Non-factorizability" paragraph's own conditional is a separate [C] claim).
